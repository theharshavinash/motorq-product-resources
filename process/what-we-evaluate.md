# What we evaluate

We score every candidate against the same six dimensions across the loop. We are publishing the dimensions and what strong looks like in each, because knowing what we value should raise the quality of your application, not let you game it — there is no way to fake a named user or a verified number. The internal scoring anchors are ours; the standards below are yours to meet.

No single stage tests one dimension in isolation, and no candidate is expected to be equally strong in all six. We are looking for a credible whole, not a perfect score.

---

## D1 · Problem framing and product sense

Strong candidates interrogate the problem before reaching for a solution. They separate the user from the buyer, take an unglamorous operational workflow seriously, and can say who has the problem, how they know, and why it is worth solving now. When they cut scope, the cut has a reason behind it.

## D2 · Data judgment and analytical rigor

Strong candidates distrust a number before they present it. They notice mixed units, duplicates, and nulls that mean "not applicable" rather than "missing"; they size their claims; and they can go from raw, messy data to a defensible recommendation without being led there. A confident recommendation built on an unchecked number is the failure mode here.

## D3 · Building with AI

Strong candidates use AI tools as an extension of their judgment, not a replacement for it. They narrate why a given scope, what they verified in the model's output, and what they deliberately left out. Fluency in one tool used well beats a tour of five. A polished artifact produced without visible reasoning does not clear this bar.

## D4 · Technical fluency

Strong candidates hold a credible tradeoff conversation with an engineer: freshness against cost, push against pull, what "real-time" actually commits us to. They can spec something precisely enough that support and the customer would trust the same number. They do not need to write code, and they do not pretend to know what they do not.

## D5 · Communication and narration

Strong candidates write and speak for decisions, not documentation. Their one-pager ends somewhere — a recommendation, a cut, a metric that should move. They think out loud in a way an interviewer can follow, and they are honest about uncertainty rather than papering over it.

## D6 · Ownership and drive

Strong candidates have a track record of deciding without certainty, owning outcomes, and doing things unasked. They tell real stories with real numbers, briefly, including the times they were wrong. We are reading for judgment and drive, not for a polished narrative.
