# Books

Buying a stack of books is the most common way candidates waste placement season. You do not need most of these before the interview, and a few of them are actively better read after you join. The tiers below say how much to read and why. Nothing here is required.

## Read this one

- **Inspired — Marty Cagan.** The canonical description of what the job actually is: discovery over delivery, empowered teams, why most roadmaps are wrong. If you read one thing on this list, read this. Cover to cover is worth it.

## Go deeper, if the area is your weak spot

- **Continuous Discovery Habits — Teresa Torres.** The customer-conversation muscle, made concrete. Read it if talking to users is the part you have least done.
- **Escaping the Build Trap — Melissa Perri.** Outcomes over output, and why shipping features is not the same as making progress. Read it if you tend to jump to solutions.

## Chapters only

- **Designing Data-Intensive Applications — Martin Kleppmann.** Chapters 1 and 11 only, for the technical-fluency round. Enough to reason about reliability and streaming; the rest is more than a PM needs.
- **Decode and Conquer — Lewis Lin.** The frameworks, as scaffolding. Skim for structure, then throw the scripts away — reciting a framework at us reads worse than thinking out loud.
- **Storytelling with Data — Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic.** The chapters on decluttering and on which chart to use. Directly useful for the working-session one-pager.

## After you join, not before

These are good books that will not help you in the interview and can hurt you if they eat your last free month. Save them.

- **Radical Candor — Kim Scott.** People and feedback. Matters once you have a team.
- **Good Strategy Bad Strategy — Richard Rumelt.** Strategy at a level above the individual PM.
- **The Making of a Manager — Julie Zhuo.** For when you are managing, which is not year one.
